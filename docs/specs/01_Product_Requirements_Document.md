## A. Product overview

### A.1 Description

A **surface operations system for one Mars Exploration Zone** at Gale crater (Mount Sharp). It has one server and four clients, plus a presenter panel for the team that runs a session (added 2026-11-10).

- **Hab Core** is a server running inside the habitat. It holds the maps, the routing grid, the compute engine, the mission clock and the live state of every crew member, rover and vehicle. Nothing on it depends on Earth.
- **Planner** (the v1 product) is used before the EVA. A planner places science and resource stops; the system computes a suit-safe route, time, daylight, dose and a briefing card.
- **EVA client** runs on the suit's wrist/chest tablet and in a minimal **HUD mode**. During the walk it shows the next stop, the route, margins, alerts and actions: summon vehicle, task rover, abort.
- **Ops console** is used by the crew member staying in the habitat (intravehicular, IV). It shows everyone's position, consumables, rovers, conditions and alerts on one map.
- **Earth view** (desirable) is mission control on Earth, receiving delayed, store-and-forward updates. It can advise but cannot act in time during an emergency, which is why everything above it runs on Mars.
- **Presenter panel** *(added 2026-11-10, Proposed)* is the control surface of the team running a session. It is not part of the story: the ops console is the in-story habitat operator, the presenter panel triggers the beats (next stop, start the storm, clock speed, reset). Every press is a command in the event log (FR-51).

The system keeps v1's rule that **deterministic, tested code computes every number** and every value carries a provenance label.

### A.2 Problem statement

**Confirmed (challenge overview):** NASA data about Mars "isn't yet layered together in the combinations needed to plan specific human missions," and existing tools "are built for regional and global views rather than the hyperlocalized areas astronauts will target on individual spacewalks or surface excursions." The challenge asks for a tool "a Mars astronaut could use **during** a surface exploration mission," including "updates on current conditions."

**Proposed framing:** a crew on a Marswalk needs to answer three questions, in order of urgency:

1. *Am I safe right now, and how quickly can I reach shelter if that changes?*
2. *Is the plan still achievable with the margins I have left?*
3. *What should I study or sample here, and why does it matter for this mission and for Mars science?*

Earth cannot answer any of these in time: one-way light time is about 3–22 minutes (computed from the 0.37–2.68 AU Earth–Mars distance range) and the link is unavailable around solar conjunction. So the answers must come from the habitat.

### A.4 Exploration Zone framing (decided 2026-11-04)

NASA's human landing site work describes candidate sites as **Exploration Zones** containing *science regions of interest* and *resource regions of interest*. Our Gale site is presented the same way (**Proposed**; NASA EZ terminology **Unresolved — confirm citation** in TASK-04).

| Stop type | Example objective at Gale | Why it matters to an early crew | Data shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Science — climate history** | Mount Sharp's layers record a change from clay-bearing (wetter) to sulfate-bearing (drier) units | How and when Mars lost its surface water: the planet-scale story the mission exists to read | HiRISE, CRISM (if covered), Curiosity images |
| **Science — groundwater** | Boxwork ridges: fractures mineralised by ancient groundwater | How long liquid water persisted underground; habitability window | HiRISE, DEM, Curiosity 2025 campaign images |
| **Resource — hydrated minerals** | Clay- and sulfate-bearing units as a possible water source for ISRU | Water for life support and propellant is the first resource a base needs. Hydrated minerals yield less than ice but Gale has no shallow ice | CRISM (if covered), thermal inertia, Curiosity results |
| **Engineering — terrain** | Sand/loose-fines patches, slopes, rock fields near the hab | Trafficability for future vehicles and construction | Thermal inertia, slope, rover scout images |

Every POI states its stop type, objective, and the evidence behind it.

## C. Scope

Tiers replace v1's single MVP list. A tier is not started until the one above it meets its exit criteria (06 §B).

### C.1 Tier 0 — Planning spine (Must; = v1 MVP, trimmed)

- One site (Gale), one AOI, local-only data.
- Map with optical basemap, hillshade/elevation, slope, thermal inertia; legends; provenance.
- Point inspection; POIs with ground images and stop type (A.4).
- Waypoints; constraint-aware routing; profile; time, distance, daylight (Mars24), radiation background estimate.
- Briefing card + plan JSON export. Sources & methods.

### C.2 Tier 1 — Surface ops MVP (Must; what the killer demo needs)

- Hab Core server with session state, mission clock and WebSocket fan-out.
- EVA client with HUD mode; ops console.
- Simulated two-person EVA progressing along the planned route; suit consumables.
- Replayed space-weather event (DONKI + RAD), Earth-delayed forecast, local radiation trigger.
- Time to safe haven (hab + pressurized rover); pressurized rover summon with rendezvous.
- Comm coverage layer and comm-shadow warnings.
- Earth link model (light time, conjunction, relay pass windows) shown as a status widget.
- Contingency re-plan (abort to haven, skip remaining stops).
- Event log and post-EVA report.
- Stop task list on the EVA tablet, synced to ops (FR-50, added 2026-11-05).
- Full-screen map on the EVA tablet with on-screen map controls, and zoom/pan buttons on the ops and planner maps (FR-32, added 2026-11-05).
- Robotic rover tasking — scout, carry, relay, return — including the rover as a comm relay that closes a shadow (FR-41; moved from Tier 2 on 2026-11-05).
- Live operations session *(added 2026-11-10, Proposed)*: presenter role and panel with Next stop, Start storm, clock control and Reset (FR-51); the storm started on demand at the current sim time (FR-52); mission site chosen from pre-checked candidates at setup (FR-53); a per-POI stop task library (FR-54).

### C.3 Tier 2 — Should (only after Tier 1 exit criteria)

- Terrain radiation refuge (sky-view factor) as a third shelter option.
- Earth support view with store-and-forward queue.
- CRISM resource layer (if AOI coverage exists); REMS seasonal context.
- Dust-storm advisory replay from orbiter weather imagery (qualitative).

### C.4 Post-MVP

- Free placement of the hab with a live 20 m viewshed (until then the hab is chosen from the pre-checked candidates, FR-53); additional sites; 3D terrain; voice interaction; AI narration of the event log (grounded only in logged values); real AR headset.

### C.5 Out of scope

- Real telemetry, real radio links, or any operational claim.
- Accounts, cloud, billing, internet at runtime.
- Medical or flight-rule radiation limits; suit certification claims.
- Joystick/real-time driving of vehicles (realistic surface ops use goal-level commands).
- AI-generated numbers of any kind.

### D.2 New requirements

#### FR-30 — Hab Core session server [T1, M]

- **User story:** As the crew, we have one authoritative source of mission state inside the habitat that works with no link to Earth.
- **Behavior:** Serves all clients and site assets on the local network; holds the active plan, mission clock, crew/vehicle/rover state and event log; broadcasts state changes to subscribed clients; persists the session to disk.
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - With the internet disconnected and devices on the local router only, all four clients load from the Hab Core and receive state updates.
  - A client that reconnects receives the current full state snapshot, then deltas.
  - Restarting the Hab Core resumes the session from disk at the last logged event.

#### FR-31 — Client roles [T1, M]

- Clients join with a role: `planner`, `eva:EV1`, `eva:EV2`, `ops`, `earth`, and `presenter` (added 2026-11-10, FR-51). Roles determine which actions are allowed (e.g., only `ops` approves a re-plan; `eva` can trigger abort and summon; `earth` can only send messages through the delayed link).
- **Acceptance:** an action from a role without permission is rejected with a visible reason and logged.

#### FR-32 — EVA client (suit tablet) [T1, M]

- **User story:** As an EVA crew member, I see where to go next, how much margin I have, and what to do if something changes, on a small screen with gloves on.
- **Full-screen map** *(added 2026-11-05, Tier 1, product & systems lead's decision)*: tapping the mini-map or FULL opens a full-screen map with labelled on-screen controls (+, −, FIT, FOLLOW, LAYERS, CLOSE) and up to two scientific layers at once (slope, thermal inertia, comm shadow, haven bands). HAVEN and O₂ stay on screen, the alert slot and action row never move, and a warning closes it. Layout: 04 §E.5; prototype: [mockup handoff](../design/mockup-handoff.md) DV-13, G-42, G-43.
- **Content:** next stop (name, bearing, distance, ETA), mini-map with route and my position, margins strip (time to haven, O₂ remaining, EVA time remaining, dose so far), alert banner, three primary actions: **Summon vehicle**, **Task rover**, **Abort to haven**.
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - Works on a phone/tablet ≥ 360 px wide in portrait; touch targets ≥ 56 px (glove use, **Assumed**).
  - Each primary action is ≤ 2 taps + confirm.
  - When the Hab Core link is lost, shows "HAB LINK LOST" and continues to display the cached plan and last known margins, labelled stale with age.

#### FR-33 — HUD mode [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** A minimal, high-contrast layout of the EVA client simulating a visor display: bearing to next stop, distance, time to haven, O₂ margin, one alert line. No map unless requested.
- **Acceptance:** all HUD values readable in ≤ 3 s by test users (B.2); passes contrast ≥ 7 : 1 against its background.

#### FR-34 — Mission clock and scenario replay [T1, M]

- **User story:** As a demonstrator, I run a reproducible EVA at a real date with real data, faster than real time.
- **Behavior:** A scenario file sets the start date-time (default **2024-05-20, sol 4190**, D-10), the plan, crew, assets and scheduled events. The clock runs at selectable speed (1×, 10×, 60×), can pause, and drives every time-dependent value. Ops can jump to the next event.
- *Live session (changed 2026-11-10, Proposed):* the scenario file carries the mission setup and an event library instead of a fixed plan and timeline (05 Scenario); the presenter role has the clock and jump controls too (FR-51). Same scenario + seed + data version **+ the same recorded command list** produce an identical event-log hash.
- **Acceptance:** two runs of the same scenario + seed + data version produce identical event logs (hash).

#### FR-35 — Crew progress and suit consumables [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** EV1 and EV2 advance along the planned route using the v1 walking-time model and dwell times; each suit tracks O₂ remaining, battery, and dose received. Buddy rule: separation between EV1 and EV2 ≤ 200 m (**Assumed**), warning if exceeded.
- **Labels:** position and consumables are `simulated`; the consumption rate is `modelled` from Apollo-era metabolic planning values (02 D.6); suit capacity is `assumed`.
- **Acceptance:** at 60×, O₂ remaining decreases consistently with the stated model (hand check at 3 timestamps).

#### FR-36 — Conditions feed [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** A conditions panel on ops and a compact strip on EVA: local solar time, sun elevation, background dose rate, current event dose rate (during replay), Earth link status. Temperature/pressure from REMS climatology if FR-13 is built; otherwise "not included in this build."
- **Acceptance:** every value shows its provenance class; nothing is labelled "live."

#### FR-37 — Space-weather alert replay [T1, M]

- **User story:** As a crew, we react to a real solar event that actually reached Mars.
- **Behavior:** The scenario replays a historical event using (a) DONKI records for the flare/CME/SEP and the WSA-ENLIL prediction of arrival at Mars, and (b) the Curiosity RAD dose for that event. Default: the **20 May 2024** event, the largest surge RAD had measured since landing, with a total dose of about **8,100 µGy** for a person standing next to the rover (NASA, June 2024 — **Confirmed** via secondary report; replace with nasa.gov URL in TASK-04). *(Changed 2026-11-10: the event starts when the presenter presses Start storm, at the current sim time; FR-52.)* The alert timeline distinguishes:
  1. local radiation trigger on Mars (FR-38),
  2. Earth-side forecast, which arrives late because of light time and relay windows (FR-43).
- **Acceptance:** event onset, peak and alert arrival times in the app match the scenario table built from the source records; the dose curve is labelled `replayed` with its source (or "digitised from published figure" if RAD time series is unavailable, U-15).

#### FR-38 — Local radiation trigger [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** The Hab Core monitors the (replayed) surface dose rate. When it exceeds the alert threshold (default 10 × background, **Assumed**), it raises `RADIATION_ALERT`, starts a shelter timer, and computes shelter options for each crew member.
- **Acceptance:** the alert fires at the first timestep where the threshold is crossed; all clients show it within 500 ms.

#### FR-39 — Time to safe haven [T0/T1, M]

- **User story:** As crew, I always know how many minutes I am from pressurized shelter, and the plan never takes me beyond the limit.
- **Behavior:** For every point on the route and for each crew member's current position, compute minimum time to reach (a) the hab on foot, (b) a rendezvous with the pressurized rover (FR-40), (c) a terrain refuge (FR-47, T2). Take the minimum. In planning, flag any segment where it exceeds the haven rule.
- **Acceptance:** for 5 test positions, the value matches an independent calculation on the same grid within 1 min.

#### FR-40 — Pressurized rover summon and rendezvous [T1, M]

- **User story:** As crew, I call the pressurized rover and it meets me at the fastest reachable point.
- **Behavior:** The rover routes on the grid with its own parameters (02 D.9). The rendezvous point minimises the time until both crew and rover are there. The EVA client shows the rendezvous point, both ETAs and the vehicle's route; ops sees the same. The rover moves only after the crew confirms (or ops confirms on their behalf).
- **Acceptance:** rendezvous is reachable by both; the rover's path never violates its slope limit; ETA is shown with its assumptions (speed, slope limit).

#### FR-41 — Robotic rover tasking [T1, M]

*(Moved from Tier 2 on 2026-11-05 by the product & systems lead so the relay moment is in the dry run and the 240-s video.)*

- **Tasks:** `SCOUT(point)` (drive, capture curated imagery, report terrain class), `CARRY(item, to)`, `RELAY(point)` (park to extend radio coverage), `RETURN`.
- **Behavior:** goal-level commands only; the rover routes autonomously; battery is its consumable. Tasks queue and show ETA.
- **Acceptance:** a `RELAY` task placed on a ridge removes a comm shadow on the remaining route (FR-42) and the warnings update.

#### FR-42 — Comm coverage and comm shadows [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** From the hab antenna (and any relay rover), compute line-of-sight coverage over the DEM including Mars curvature. Show a coverage layer. Route segments without coverage get a `COMM_SHADOW` warning; optionally add a routing penalty.
- **Context:** on a smooth sphere of Mars' radius, a 2 m-high suit antenna sees a 10 m mast only to about 3.7 + 8.2 ≈ 11.9 km (computed). At Mount Sharp, terrain blocks far sooner, which is what this layer shows.
- **Acceptance:** coverage at 5 test points matches an independent LOS check; the layer is labelled `derived` with "radio propagation not modelled beyond line of sight."

#### FR-43 — Earth link model [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** For the scenario date, show one-way light time, whether the link is available (relay pass windows over Gale, solar conjunction), and a store-and-forward queue for messages in both directions. Values come from precomputed SPICE geometry (02 D.12).
- **Acceptance:** light time matches SPICE for the scenario date within 1 s; messages sent while no relay is overhead are queued and delivered at the next pass.

#### FR-44 — Contingency re-plan [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** On an alert or a crew request, propose options ranked by time to shelter and dose: abort to hab, rendezvous with vehicle, (T2) terrain refuge, or continue with skipped stops. Ops approves (or the crew acts directly on an abort).
- **Acceptance:** each option shows ETA, expected dose, and which stops are dropped; the chosen option becomes the new active plan with a new revision.

#### FR-45 — Exploration Zone layer [T0, M]

- **Behavior:** POIs and regions are typed as science / resource / engineering (A.4) with distinct icons; the layer legend explains each type.
- **Acceptance:** every POI has `stop_type`, objective, evidence and source; at least one resource POI exists in the demo plan.

#### FR-46 — Event log and post-EVA report [T1, M]

- **Behavior:** Every state change is logged (time, actor, event, values, provenance). After the EVA, a report extends the briefing card: planned vs. actual timeline, alerts and responses, dose received vs. planned, samples/stops completed.
- **Acceptance:** report prints on ≤ 2 pages; log export (JSON) reproduces the run when replayed.

#### FR-47 — Terrain radiation refuge [T2, S]

- **Behavior:** Compute a sky-view factor from the DEM; rank nearby cells where terrain blocks the most sky (e.g., below a cliff) as partial shelter. The RAD principal investigator has noted that cliffsides or lava tubes would add shielding during such events (**Confirmed**, secondary report). Dose reduction is a simplified `modelled` value with stated limits.
- **Acceptance:** refuge option appears in FR-44 with its reduction factor and "simplified isotropic model" label.

#### FR-48 — Earth support view [T2, S]

- Delayed telemetry stream and message composer; everything sent waits in the FR-43 queue.

#### FR-49 — Dust advisory replay [T2, S]

- Qualitative advisory replayed from orbiter weather imagery records for the scenario window, if a suitable event is found (U-17). Never quantitative.

#### FR-50 — Stop task list on the EVA tablet, synced to ops [T1, M]

*(Added 2026-11-05 at the product & systems lead's request; **Proposed** until team sign-off.)*

- **User story:** As an EVA crew member, I see the short list of things to do at the stop I am at, tick them off with gloves on, and know that ops sees the same list. As ops, I see each crew member's progress per stop and can add a task to a crew member's current or next stop.
- **Behavior:**
  - Each planned stop carries 1–4 **stop tasks** (05 StopTask): a kind (`observe | image | sample | measure | deploy | other`) and one line of text written by the team from the POI's objective (FR-45). Task text is authored content, not computed; any number in it must cite its source like any other number.
  - The EVA tablet shows the current stop's tasks while the crew member is at the stop, and a count (for example "Stop 2 · tasks 1/3") while walking. A task goes `TODO → DONE` or `TODO → SKIPPED` (reason: time, terrain, alert, other) with one tap and a 5-s undo; no hold-to-confirm, because it is not a safety action.
  - Each change is a command to the Hab Core (`UPDATE_STOP_TASK`), logged as an event and broadcast, so ops, the HUD count and the post-EVA report all show the same state. Without a hab link the change is queued like other commands (02 §E.6) and marked "not synced".
  - Ops can add a task (`ADD_STOP_TASK`) to a crew member's current or next stop; it appears on that tablet with an "added by ops" tag.
  - When a plan revision drops a stop (abort, skip remaining stops, FR-44), its open tasks become `DROPPED` automatically (actor `system`). A crew member not driven by a person (for example EV2 in the demo) completes tasks at the end of each dwell, so headless replays stay deterministic (NFR-06).
  - The briefing card (FR-14) lists each stop's tasks; the post-EVA report (FR-46) shows done, skipped and dropped tasks per stop.
- **Provenance:** task state is `simulated` (crew actions inside a simulated EVA); task text carries its author and the POI source.
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - A tap on the tablet appears on the ops console and on the other devices within the LAN broadcast budget of the TASK-20 spike (≤ 500 ms).
  - Abort during the killer demo turns the remaining stops' open tasks into `DROPPED` on every device.
  - The report's task counts equal the counts reduced from the event log.
  - Task controls never move the alert slot, HAVEN, O₂ or the action row (04 §A.3 fixed geography); during an alert the list collapses to its count.

#### FR-51 — Live session and presenter panel [T1, M]

*(Added 2026-11-10 at the product & systems lead's decision; **Proposed** until team sign-off. Exact command names and payloads are **Proposed**.)*

- **User story:** As the team running the session, we drive the demo from one panel: we move the crew to the next stop, start the storm when we choose, change the clock speed and reset, without scripts and without touching the in-story ops console.
- **Behavior:**
  - A `presenter` role and panel (SCR-19), separate from the ops console. The ops console is in-story (the habitat operator); the presenter panel is the team's control surface.

It is not a teleport: crew keep walking along the planned route under the hood, and consumables, dose, alerts and time accrue normally (02 §D.17). If an alert is raised on the way, the fast-forward stops at it (`TODO(decision)`, U-63).

## Appendix A — v1 requirement text (FR-01–FR-21)

### FR-01 — Load site and optical basemap offline

- **User story:** As a planner, I open the app and immediately see the excursion site in high-resolution orbital imagery.
- **Priority / release:** M / MVP
- **Behavior:** On launch, load `site-manifest.json`, initialise the local Mars projection, render the HiRISE-based basemap at the AOI extent.
- **Inputs / outputs:** Site manifest → rendered map, scale bar in metres, north arrow.
- **Dependencies:** TASK-01 (AOI), TASK-03 (tiles).
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - *Given* the network is disabled, *when* the app is opened from the local server, *then* the basemap renders within 5 s and no network request leaves `localhost`.
  - *Given* the map at any zoom, *then* the scale bar length agrees with a measured feature (e.g., known crater diameter in the DEM) within 2 %.

### FR-02 — Scientific layers with legend and opacity

- **User story:** As a planner, I toggle elevation, slope and thermal-inertia layers to understand terrain and surface materials.
- **Priority:** M (optical, hillshade, elevation, slope, thermal inertia); S (CRISM spectral — FR-18)
- **Behavior:** Layer panel lists each layer with on/off, opacity (0–100 %), legend with units, resolution, and provenance label.
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - *Given* any layer is on, *then* its legend shows units (m, °, tiu) and native resolution.
  - Toggling a layer updates the map within 300 ms (tiles cached).
  - Layer order is fixed: basemap < hillshade < scientific overlay < hazards < route < POIs.

### FR-03 — Point inspection

- **User story:** As a planner, I click a spot to read its exact position and values.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Click/tap (or the keyboard crosshair, see NFR-04 and [04 §E](04_Design_Brief.md#f-accessibility-and-responsive-behavior)) shows a popover: planetocentric latitude, east-positive longitude (°, 5 decimals), elevation (m relative to the DEM's MOLA-tied datum), slope (°), thermal inertia (tiu or "no data"), each with source.
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - Values match the preprocessed rasters at that cell (test: 10 sampled cells against Python `rasterio` readout, tolerance: exact for elevation at routing-grid cell, ±0.1° slope).
  - Clicking outside the AOI shows "Outside mapped area" and no values.

### FR-04 — Points of interest with ground imagery

- **User story:** As a crew member, I see what a science stop looks like from the ground before I go.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** POI markers (5–8 curated) open a detail panel (SCR-06): name, science objective, why it matters, a locally stored Curiosity Navcam/Mastcam image with credit, sol/date, approximate look direction if known, and "Add as stop" button.

  - Every POI has `image_credit`, `source_url`, and `position_source` fields populated (see [05](05_Backend_and_Database_Schema.md#poi)).
  - "Add as stop" appends the POI as the next waypoint and triggers recomputation.

### FR-05 — Mission inputs with validated defaults

- **User story:** As a planner, I set EVA constraints so the plan reflects my mission rules.
- **Priority:** M
- **Inputs (defaults are Assumed, editable):** EVA start (UTC date-time or local true solar time), max EVA duration (h, default 6), dwell time per stop (min, default 20), hard slope limit (°, default 15), caution slope (°, default 10), walkback limit (km of route distance from start, default 5), flat-ground suited speed (km/h, default 3.3 — Apollo 17 planning value), solar-cycle phase for radiation (max / min).

  - A visible badge says "Defaults are assumptions — not flight rules."

### FR-06 — Waypoint placement and editing

- **User story:** As a planner, I place a start point and science stops, then drag, reorder or delete them.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Start point = habitat/rover position (default from manifest, movable). Up to 10 stops. Stops are numbered; list in panel supports reorder (drag + keyboard up/down buttons) and delete. Route returns to start by default ("loop" on; toggle to one-way).
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - Placing a waypoint on a no-data or impassable cell is refused with a message and the nearest passable cell offered (snap within 50 m).
  - Undo of the last waypoint action is available (single-level is sufficient).

### FR-07 — Constraint-aware route computation

- **User story:** As a planner, I want the tool to find a walkable path between my stops that avoids slopes I cannot climb and known hazards.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** For each consecutive waypoint pair, run least-time search on the routing grid (algorithm and cost model in [02 §D.2](02_Technical_Requirements_Document.md#d2-route-planning-fr-06-fr-07-fr-10)). Cells above the hard slope limit, no-data cells and hard-hazard cells are impassable; caution cells cost more.
- **Outputs:** Polyline per leg, total distance, total time, max slope, list of flagged segments.
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - *Given* two passable points connected by passable cells, *when* routing runs, *then* a route is returned whose every step satisfies the hard slope limit (verified by re-sampling the result).
  - *Given* no passable connection, *then* the leg is marked "No feasible route" with the reason (e.g., "all paths exceed 15° slope") and the nearest reachable point toward the target is shown.
  - Routing a 3-stop loop completes in ≤ 2 s (NFR-02).
  - Same inputs + same data version ⇒ identical route (NFR-06).

### FR-08 — Elevation and slope profile

- **User story:** As a planner, I see where the climbs and dangerous sections are.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Profile chart under the map: elevation vs. cumulative distance, segments coloured *and patterned* by slope class (OK / caution / over-limit), stop markers. Hover/keyboard focus on the chart highlights the corresponding map location and vice versa.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Total climb and descent shown in metres; chart remains readable in grayscale (pattern + label, not colour only).

### FR-09 — Traverse time, distance and return estimates

- **User story:** As a planner, I know how long the excursion takes and whether I stay within limits.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Compute walking time per leg (slope-dependent speed model), add dwell times, compute EVA timeline, remaining margin vs. max duration, and maximum route distance from start (walkback check).
- **Acceptance criteria:** Reference cases in [02 §D.3](02_Technical_Requirements_Document.md#d3-walking-time-model-fr-09) reproduce exactly in unit tests (e.g., 1 km flat at 3.3 km/h = 18.2 min).

### FR-10 — Hazard and constraint warnings

- **Priority:** M
- **Warning types:** over hard slope (blocked), caution slope, low-thermal-inertia "possible loose sand" (caution, interpretive), no-data cell adjacency, walkback limit exceeded, EVA duration exceeded, segment in darkness (sun below horizon).
- **Acceptance criteria:** Each warning shows severity icon + text + affected segment/time, appears in the results panel and on the briefing card, and can be clicked to zoom to the location.

### FR-11 — Daylight and sun conditions

- **User story:** As a planner, I want to finish the walk in daylight.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Compute local true solar time, solar elevation and azimuth at the site for the EVA window using the Mars24 algorithm (Allison & McEwen 2000); show sunrise/sunset (local, ignoring terrain), L_s (season), Mars Year.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Unit tests match reference values from a trusted Mars24 implementation for ≥ 3 timestamps within 0.1° (solar elevation) and 1 min (LTST). Terrain shadowing is explicitly stated as not modelled.

### FR-12 — Radiation dose estimate

- **User story:** As a planner, I see the expected surface radiation dose for this excursion and how solid that number is.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Dose equivalent = measured Gale surface rate × EVA duration, for selected solar-cycle phase. Show rate source (Curiosity RAD), uncertainty, and caveats (no suit shielding, no SEP events, no terrain shielding).
- **Acceptance criteria:** For a 7 h EVA at solar-maximum rate 0.64 mSv/day the card shows 0.19 mSv (verified arithmetic: 0.64 × 7/24 = 0.187). Label: "measured rate × planned duration (simplified)."

### FR-13 — Environmental context (seasonal weather)

- **Priority:** S
- **Behavior:** Show REMS-derived typical air/ground temperature and pressure ranges for the L_s bin of the EVA date.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Values trace to named REMS RDR files and the binning script; if not prepared, the panel shows "Not included in this build" rather than placeholder numbers.

### FR-14 — Briefing card and plan export

- **User story:** As a crew member, I take a one-page summary of the excursion with me.
- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Print-optimised view (SCR-08): map thumbnail with route, stops table, timeline, profile, constraints, warnings, conditions, assumptions, sources, data version, plan hash. Export plan as JSON (schema in [05](05_Backend_and_Database_Schema.md#planexport)).
- **Acceptance criteria:** Prints to one A4/Letter page in Chrome without clipping; re-importing exported JSON (if FR-20 built) or reloading it in a test reproduces identical metrics.

### FR-15 — Provenance and methods

- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Every layer and computed value has a provenance label and a link into the Sources & Methods panel (SCR-09), which lists datasets, versions, resolution, licence/credit, processing steps and known limitations.
- **Acceptance criteria:** No numeric value is displayed without a provenance label (automated UI test checks `data-provenance` attribute on every metric element).

### FR-16 — Offline and degraded operation

- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** All demo assets are local. If a layer fails to load, the layer row shows an error state and the rest of the app works; routing requires only the routing grid.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Deleting the thermal-inertia tiles results in a visible "Layer unavailable" state and routing still succeeds (sand-caution warnings then show "unknown").

### FR-17 — Curiosity traverse overlay

- **Priority:** M
- **Behavior:** Show Curiosity's historical traverse and waypoints in/near the AOI with sol labels; clicking shows sol and source.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Overlay aligns with visible rover tracks on the HiRISE basemap at ≥ 3 checked locations (tolerance stated in [02 §C](02_Technical_Requirements_Document.md#c-mars-data-and-geospatial-handling)).

### FR-18 — Spectral (CRISM) layer — S

- Shown only if TASK-01 confirms MTRDR coverage of the AOI. Labelled as summary-parameter browse product, "not a mineral identification."

### FR-19 — Illustrative consumables estimate — S

- Metabolic-rate-based oxygen estimate from historical planning values; labelled **illustrative**, never shown as a limit.

### FR-20 — Save/restore plan locally — S

- Last plan in browser storage, wrapped in error handling; app works if storage is unavailable.

### FR-21 — Post-MVP items — C

- Space-weather status, 3D view, multi-site, AI narration (see C.3).
